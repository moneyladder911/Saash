## THE PRINCIPLE BEHIND EVERY REWRITE

The current deck's failure mode is consistent: **it asserts where it could evidence, and it omits where the omission is louder than the answer.** Every rewrite below does the same three things:

1. **Concede the thing the investor will find anyway** — before they find it.
2. **Convert the concession into the argument.** "Others tried and retreated" is a *stronger* slide than "nobody thought of it," because it proves the category is real and hard.
3. **Show the sensitivity, not the point estimate.** A deck showing its own downside case reads as commercially literate. A deck showing one number reads as unexamined.

You lose nothing by doing this. Everything below is still a bullish deck.

## SLIDE 9 — FIX THE THREE CLAIMS

### Do not say → say instead

| ❌ Currently says | Why it fails | ✅ Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| "validated against the Courage + Khazaka **standard**" | The Corneometer® and Sebumeter® are trademarked instruments, not a standard. Also a trademark-use problem. | "correlated against a Courage + Khazaka Corneometer® CM 825 under [PROTOCOL], n=[N], across Fitzpatrick I–VI" |
| "the German standard" | Unsubstantiated establishment claim | Delete. Say the instrument name and let it carry the weight. |
| "measure hydration **and sebum** directly from the skin surface" | The Sebumeter is grease-spot photometry with a consumable tape. There is no established solid-state contact sebum sensor. | See below — this needs a decision, not a rewrite. |

### The sebum decision you have to make

There are only two honest versions, and **both are fine**:

**(a) It uses a consumable.** Then say so, and put it in the model — this is *good news*, because it is the recurring revenue the deck currently lacks. New line: *"Sebum measurement uses a replaceable [CONSUMABLE], [N] readings per cartridge, [PRICE]."* This single change also repairs the retention story.

**(b) It is an optical proxy, not corneometry-grade.** Then say: *"Sebum is inferred from [PRINCIPLE]. It is a directional index, not a laboratory measurement."* Losing the word "measure" costs you nothing with a serious investor and protects you from an FTC substantiation problem.

**What you cannot do** is keep "measures sebum directly from the skin surface, validated against Courage + Khazaka." A technical diligence call ends on that sentence.

### The melanin claim — keep it, narrow it

Currently overreaching, and undercut on your own slide because the camera path handles pigmentation, which is where tone bias actually lives.

> ✅ **"The hydration reading is taken by capacitance at the skin surface, so it does not degrade with melanin content — unlike camera-derived measures. Pigmentation and redness are read optically and are subject to the same tone sensitivity as the rest of the category; we [MITIGATION]."**

Conceding the camera limitation makes the capacitance claim believable. Claiming tone-independence for the whole system makes both suspect.

### The evidence gap to close before the raise

No published trial tests LED protocols adapted to hydration or sebum readings — and the best-powered relevant RCT found LED improved wrinkle volume but **not** hydration. Until you have your own pilot, the honest and still-compelling framing is:

> **"Adaptive dosing is our thesis, not yet our evidence. [N]-subject pilot running [DATES], reading out [DATE]."**

Naming an unproven thesis as unproven, with a date, is how a technical founder talks. It is worth more than a claim you cannot defend.

## TWO THINGS TO STOP SAYING

**1. "Margins that survive a 40% tariff."** True, but trivially — ex-factory is 17% of retail, so 10 points of duty costs 1.7 points of margin. Claiming it as a strength invites a stress test you pass while drawing attention to the ones you don't. *The tariff work itself is excellent and should stay on the unit economics slide — just stop framing it as a moat.*

**2. "78–83% gross margin" as a comparison to listed comps.** TBTG is >90% DTC and still 62.7%, so "we're DTC" doesn't explain it. Replace with the real explanation:

> ✅ **"We price at $790 a device whose landed cost is comparable to devices retailing at $395–650. The margin is a function of positioning, not of manufacturing advantage — and positioning is what this round tests."**

That is an answerable claim. The current one invites "why are you fifteen points better than listed companies?", which has no good answer in a first meeting.
